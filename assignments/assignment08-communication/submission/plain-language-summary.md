## plain language note

This summary is intended for public health students and health education professionals interested in how waist circumference differs across education levels among US adults aged 20–80.

The plot compares mean waist circumference across three education groups in the NHANES 1999–2000 and 2021–2023 survey cycles. Mean waist circumference increased in all three groups. College graduates had a lower mean waist circumference than adults in the Some College and High School or Less groups in both cycles. The High School or Less group showed a smaller increase between the two cycles than the other groups. These patterns raise questions about how education and social conditions relate to health.

However, this observational comparison does not prove that completing college causes a smaller waist circumference. Differences in age, income, access to healthcare, and other characteristics could help explain the patterns. Comparing two survey cycles also does not show that waist circumference increased steadily between them or that the same individuals experienced an increase.

A useful next step is to calculate the differences between education groups across all available survey cycles, include uncertainty estimates, and examine whether the patterns remain after accounting for age and other relevant characteristics. These findings can also support conversations about making health education and preventive services more accessible without assuming that education alone explains the differences.
